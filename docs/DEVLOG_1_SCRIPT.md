**[FOOTAGE: cold open — the slot reels spinning, then slamming to a stop
on a win. 8 seconds, game audio up, no narration yet.]**

**[FOOTAGE: the office, walking toward the kitchen.]**

I'm Walt Parkman. I'm seventy-one years old, and I have no game
development experience. For forty years I built data processing systems
in office cubicles — payroll, probation records, the data warehouse
behind a casino floor. Every one of those systems failed or is being
retired right now.

So I built this. It's called Leonard Sibelius. It's the story of a man
merging with an AI, and it's shaped like the only building I ever really
understood: a casino.

**[FOOTAGE: hold V in the kitchen — the two hidden doors shimmer in,
CAROUSEL OF FATES and VIDEO POKER signs visible.]**

This week the game went alpha, version 0.8. Here's what that means.
My kitchen has two hidden doors now. One goes to the Carousel of Fates —
that's a roguelite slot machine, three rounds, chip quotas, a little
shop between rounds. The other door is new this week.

**[FOOTAGE: walk through the poker door, arrive facing the cabinet,
the green glow, sit down with E.]**

Video poker. Jacks or Better, the machine that was on every floor my
data warehouse ever watched. I never got to build one back then — I was
only allowed to count what they earned. This one is mine. Real
fifty-two card deck, honest shuffle, full-pay table. If you look
closely at the ace of spades, it carries a maker's mark, the way real
decks have since 1765. Mine says SIBELIUS.

**[FOOTAGE: a full poker hand — deal, HELD tags going up, draw, a win.
Make sure "the house suggests" line is readable.]**

And because I only played cards a few times as a child, the machine
teaches you. Every hand, the house tells you what it would keep. That
advice isn't a guess — it's the same strategy the test harness ran two
hundred thousand hands through. Follow it and you get back ninety-seven
cents on the dollar. I know because I measured it, which is the one
part of casino work I was always allowed to do.

**[FOOTAGE: the Carousel panel — HOW TO PLAY visible, then a lever pull
or two, chips climbing.]**

The whole game runs on one currency, sauce. You earn it reading books
and slapping demons, and you risk it on these machines.

**[FOOTAGE: the wild refactor — R turns something into an animal — and
one slap. The comedy beat. Let it breathe.]**

There's also this. I don't apologize for it.

**[FOOTAGE: back in the office, or the cathedral machine if you have
footage of it.]**

One more thing, because it's the honest thing. I didn't hand-write this
game. An AI wrote the code — thousands of lines of C++ — and I reviewed
it, tested it, argued with it, and told it when things were still
horrible. Today it shipped two versions before dinner. Forty years ago
I was a farmer with a plow. This is what a tractor feels like.

The game is free, it's in alpha, and the link is below. If you play one
thing, play a hand of poker and let the house teach you. Next time:
a wheel with the names of every employer I ever had, and what each of
them pays out.

**[FOOTAGE: end on the ace of spades, or the SIBELIUS maker's mark.
Hold 3 seconds. Cut.]**
